# Specialist rainwater drainage subcontracting (IE/UK): operations primer

The vocabulary below is what an Operations Director at a siphonic/gravity rainwater specialist would use day to day. Statutory points are summarised for demo copy and are not legal advice.

## 1. Applications for payment and valuations

**Monthly cycle (typical)**
- The main contractor sets a **valuation date / cut-off** (commonly the 20th-25th of the month). The sub's **application for payment (AfP)** is due by that date, usually with a cumulative valuation.
- AfP layout: **gross cumulative value**, made up of contract works % complete by line item/area/roof zone, **approved variations**, **materials on site (MOS)**, and occasionally **off-site materials** (prefab spools held in the shop, which need vesting certificates or bonds). From that, deduct **retention**, then **previous certified**, which gives **net due this period**. Add VAT / reverse charge.
- Main contractor QS **valuation / certificate** comes back. Disputes typically involve % complete, unagreed variations, **contra-charges** (cleaning, damage, attendance, scaffold hire), and **discounts** (e.g. 2.5% MC discount in UK).
- Supporting evidence: marked-up roof plans/drawings, photos, daywork sheets, delivery dockets, test certificates.

**UK: Housing Grants, Construction and Regeneration Act 1996 as amended by LDEDC Act 2009 ("Construction Act") + Scheme for Construction Contracts**
- Key dates: **due date**, then **payment notice** (payer, within 5 days of the due date), then **pay less notice** (no later than the prescribed period before the **final date for payment**; the Scheme default is 7 days), then the final date for payment (Scheme default **17 days after the due date**).
- If the payer serves no payment notice, the sub's **application acts as a "default payment notice"**, so the applied sum becomes the **notified sum** unless a valid pay less notice is served. Late or no pay less notice means the full notified sum is payable ("smash and grab" adjudication).
- **Pay-when-paid is banned** (except on upstream insolvency). There is a statutory right to **suspend for non-payment** (7 days' notice) and a right to **adjudicate at any time** (28-day decision).
- Sources: https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/w/Payment_notice_for_construction_contracts , https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/w/Pay_less_notice , https://www.payapps.com/blogs-uk/managing-the-construction-payment-process-applications-for-payment-payment-notices-and-the-construction-act/

**Ireland: Construction Contracts Act 2013 (in force 25 July 2016)**
- Contracts must set out the amount or a mechanism, payment claim dates and periods. The Schedule default applies if the contract is silent (claims at 30-day intervals; payment due 30 days after the payment claim date).
- Sub issues a **payment claim notice** no later than **5 days after the payment claim date**. The payer must respond within **21 days** with a written response if paying less, stating the amount and the reasons. Otherwise it cannot withhold.
- **Pay-when-paid is banned** (except on upstream insolvency). There is a right to **suspend** on 7 days' notice if an adjudicator's decision isn't paid, and a right to refer **payment disputes to adjudication** at any time (7-day notice of intention; decision within 28 days, extendable).
- Exclusions: contracts under **EUR 10,000**, PPP and some residential.
- Tax: **RCT (Relevant Contracts Tax)**, where the principal deducts 0/20/35% and notifies Revenue via ROS (deduction authorisation). Also **VAT reverse charge** (13.5%) on subcontract construction services. UK equivalents are **CIS** (0/20/30%) and the **domestic reverse charge VAT**.
- Forms: public jobs use **PWC** (Public Works Contracts) and the **CIF/SCS-style subcontract forms**. Private jobs use RIAI forms or bespoke MC subcontracts. UK forms are **JCT DOM/2, JCT Design & Build Sub-Contract (DBSub)** and **NEC4 ECS** (compensation events, early warnings).
- Sources: https://www.williamfry.com/knowledge/construction-contracts-act-2013-signed-into-law-what-do-you-need-to-know/ , https://lawsociety.ie/gazette/in-depth/construction-contracts-and-adjudication , https://mondaq.com/ireland/construction-planning/1009628/a-practical-guide-to-adjudication-under-the-construction-contracts-act-2013

## 2. Retentions

- Typically **3-5%** held off each payment (often 5% reducing to 2.5%, or 3%). **Half is released at practical completion** (UK) or substantial completion (Ireland). The other half is released at the end of the **defects liability / rectification period** (usually 12 months, sometimes 24).
- Pain points: sub's PC is tied to the **main contract PC**, so a roof package finished in month 6 waits for building PC in month 24. Release then needs chasing and tracking, and retention is lost on MC insolvency. Some subs offer **retention bonds** instead.
- Demo terminology: "retention ledger", "release date (half on PC, balance on end of DLP)", "retention outstanding by job/MC".

## 3. Variations

- Instructions come as **site instructions (SI)**, **architect's/engineer's instructions (AI/EI)**, **RFIs** leading to change, or (NEC) **compensation events**. The sub raises a **variation quotation**, which becomes an agreed **variation order (VO)**.
- Typical rainwater variations: additional outlets, re-routes around services clashes (BIM coordination), extra overflow/emergency outlets, gutter changes, roof falls changed by roofer, extra MEWP/scaffold days, **dayworks** (signed daywork sheets), abortive visits / return visits when the area is not ready (roofer not finished, membrane not down).
- Commercial risk: unsigned dayworks, unpriced instructed work, notice time-bars in subcontracts (often 7-14 days).

## 4. BCAR (Ireland): ancillary certificates

- Under **Building Control (Amendment) Regulations 2014 (BC(A)R / "BCAR")** the **Assigned Certifier** collates **Ancillary Certificates** from designers, specialist subcontractors and suppliers to support the **Certificate of Compliance on Completion**.
- A design-and-install drainage specialist typically provides an **Ancillary Certificate of Design** and an **Ancillary Certificate of Compliance on Completion** (works complied with Part H / Building Regs). The certificate is **signed by a principal/director** and usually comes with a supporting pack: as-built drawings, hydraulic calcs, test records, O&M manual, product certs.
- Templates/guidance come from ACEI, SCSI and RIAI. An **annex** lists inspections and evidence.
- Sources: https://scsi.ie/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Practice-Note-2-Ancillary-Certificates-Further-Guidance.pdf , https://www.acei.ie/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Risk-Advice-note-Guidance-on-the-Completion-of-the-Annex-to-Ancillary-Certificate-of-Compliance-on-Completion-v02.pdf
- UK analogue: **O&M manuals / Building Regs Approved Document H** compliance evidence, and the **Building Safety Act golden thread** for HRBs.

## 5. Standards: BS EN 12056-3 and BS 8490

- **BS EN 12056-3**: gravity drainage systems inside buildings, Part 3: roof drainage, layout and calculation. It covers hydraulic adequacy of gravity (non-siphonic) roof drainage and performance requirements for siphonic systems.
- **BS 8490** (Guide to siphonic roof drainage systems; **2007 edition superseded by BS 8490:2025**): design parameters, installation, verification and **maintenance**. The 2025 revision tightens design, inspection and maintenance expectations. https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/products/siphonic-roof-drainage-systems-guide
- **Maintenance practice**: planned inspections, typically at least annually and often 6-monthly plus after major storms. Tasks include clearing outlets and gutters, checking outlet baffles/anti-vortex plates, leaf guards, fixings and brackets, pipe supports, expansion joints, leaks and **overflows / emergency outlets**, and recording condition with photos. Customers get a **maintenance report** and a remedial quote. PTSG markets a "siphonic health check" in the UK: https://www.ptsg.co.uk/ptsg-launches-new-siphonic-health-check-tool-find-out-in-minutes-is-your-buildings-drainage-system-a-hidden-risk/
- **Also relevant**: BS EN 1253 (gullies/outlets), Part H (IE) / Approved Document H (UK), and roof falls and gutter design (BS EN 12056-3 gutters).

## 6. Siphonic vs gravity: install and test

- **Siphonic**: fewer, smaller-bore outlets and pipes run **full bore (no air)**. Collector pipes run **level, with no fall**, at high level and drop to fewer stacks, so there is less below-ground drainage. Typical materials are **HDPE** (Geberit Pluvia, Wavin QuickStream, Akasison, Fullflow), **butt-fusion and electrofusion** joints, and **suspension/rail bracket systems** that take the thrust loads. Hydraulic design is done in proprietary software, with design calcs per system.
- **Gravity**: more outlets and larger pipes, laid to falls, running part-full. Common with gutters and cast iron, steel, PVC or HDPE.
- **Hybrid**: a mix, e.g. siphonic primary system plus a gravity overflow (Capcon's job ads mention "hybrid" installs).
- **Prefabrication**: HDPE spools are welded in the shop to BIM model drawings, labelled by roof zone or grid line, then delivered and installed. Spool tracking and MOS valuations matter here.
- **Testing and commissioning**:
  - Joint integrity / leak test: outlets plugged, system **filled with water and held** while joints are inspected. On some specs, a **pressure (hydrostatic) test** of HDPE pipework, or an **air test** for gravity pipework.
  - Weld records: fusion weld logs with welder ID, machine and parameters, plus visual inspection of beads.
  - Outlet installation sign-off with the **roofing contractor** (clamping ring / membrane interface). Flow or "flood" tests are done where specified.
  - Outputs: **test certificate**, as-built drawings, commissioning record, O&M manual, BCAR ancillary cert (IE).
- Frequent site issues: roof not ready (outlet-membrane sequencing), clashes with M&E at high level, access (MEWP availability), and design changes from the roof build-up.

## 7. Technician tickets and competencies

**Ireland**
- **Safe Pass** (SOLAS, mandatory for site, renew every 4 years).
- **Manual Handling** (QQI, renew ~3 years) and **Working at Height** / harness training.
- **CSCS Ireland** cards for specific plant/tasks (e.g. slinger/signaller).
- **IPAF** (MEWP operator: 3a scissor, 3b boom; renew 5 years), **PASMA** (mobile tower; 5 years), **Abrasive Wheels**.
- Others: **first aid / OFA**, **fire warden**, and **confined space** where needed.
- Supervisors: **SSSTS/SMSTS**-type courses or **CIF Site Safety Rep**.

**UK**
- **CSCS card** (or affiliated **JIB-PMES** card for plumbing/mechanical), **CITB HS&E test**, IPAF, PASMA, SSSTS/SMSTS for supervisors, asbestos awareness.

**Pain point**: tracking expiries across a mobile workforce working both IE and UK. A main contractor's induction will turn a technician away with an expired IPAF or Safe Pass.

**Product-specific**: manufacturer **HDPE welding / installer certification** (e.g. Geberit or Wavin installer training).

## 8. RAMS, permits and induction (pharma, med-tech, data centres)

- **Legal frameworks**: IE uses the Safety, Health and Welfare at Work (Construction) Regulations 2013 (**PSDP / PSCS** roles, Safety & Health Plan). UK uses **CDM 2015** (Principal Designer / Principal Contractor, CPP).
- **RAMS** (Risk Assessment and Method Statement): task-specific (e.g. "Installation of siphonic outlets and HDPE collector pipework at high level from MEWP"). These are submitted to the MC for **approval before starting** (often 1-2 weeks lead time) and **briefed to operatives with sign-on sheets**. Revisions are needed when scope or method changes.
- **Permits to work (PTW)**: daily or shift permits for **working at height**, **hot works** (some sites class fusion welding as hot works), **roof access**, **MEWP / lifting** (lift plans for long spools), and **isolations / LOTO** in live areas. Pharma and data centre sites often run **permit issuers** and a **daily coordination meeting**.
- **Pharma / med-tech** (e.g. Dexcom Athenry, Cork/Dublin pharma clusters): site-specific inductions, sometimes **GMP / cleanroom** protocols in the later stages, strict housekeeping, and the main contractor's own safety programmes. Safety observation cards are common.
- **Data centres** (Dublin/Meath clusters, UK Slough/London): fast-track programmes where the roof drainage package is critical to **"weathertight" / "dry-in"** milestones, multiple identical halls (repeatable prefab), very strict PPE and permit regimes, **Level 1-5 commissioning** culture, and heavy QA documentation.
- Daily artefacts: **toolbox talks**, **daily task briefings / Take 5s**, **pre-use plant checks** (MEWP inspection), and incident or near-miss reports.
